## 1. Summary

Cadenza and the CPG are complementary along a single axis: **time against harmony**.

- **Cadenza** writes and performs in time: three lanes that listen to each other, sixteen steps a bar, rhythm libraries,
  JAM blocks, phrase forms, call and answer, locks, song parts, placement from MPC's song position. Its harmony, as the
  Force's screens expose it, is small: 16 progressions of diatonic degrees, the scale's seven chords plus three borrowed
  ones in the bar editor, 7 scales, and chord names spelled with sharps in every key (E♭ major reads
  `D# Fm Gm G# A# Cm Ddim`).
- **The CPG** is the mirror image: 184 genre templates in 22 genres, a strict numeral grammar with secondary dominants,
  Neapolitan, augmented sixths and tritone substitutes, a 16-pad palette per template whose fourth row is chosen by
  harmonic-function analysis, six voicing treatments, 34 scales, key-aware spelling, a CI theory audit. It has no notion
  of time: a template is a list of chords of equal weight.
- **The Ouarpeggiator** was an attempt to give the CPG a time axis. Cadenza's time axis is already far richer than the
  Ouarpeggiator is likely to become; the economical move is the converse one, bringing the CPG's harmony into Cadenza.

Three findings shape the rest of this study.

1. **Cadenza's chord model is not the obstacle.** Since 0.28 its engine stores chords on any root (major, minor, dominant,
   diminished, each in ten colours), chords of unequal length and hand-placed voicings: the "Feel" layer, built for the
   iOS app. The Force has no screen for it yet. Measured over the whole catalogue (§3), 183 of the 184 templates can be
   represented exactly or nearly, and 98.6 % of the 2 944 palette pads of the Classic variant.
2. **The obstacles are elsewhere.** The Force's progression path accepts only diatonic degrees and the three borrowed
   chords: [`fill_phrase`](../../src/seq_core.c#L339) and [`set_prog`](../../src/seq_core.c#L6268) silently turn any
   other source into a diatonic chord. Loops are 1, 2, 4, 8 or 16 bars, while 39 template entries have 3, 5, 6, 7 or
   12 chords. The skin memory budget has at most 1.2 MB of headroom (78.8 of 80 MB at its last measurement). And the
   licence forbids providing a product that competes with Cadenza.
3. **The CPG's palette is a neighbourhood; Cadenza's progression is a path.** Three operations relate them: import a
   path (a template), offer the neighbourhood wherever a chord is chosen (the palette in the bar editor), and generate
   paths from the neighbourhood (the Ouarpeggiator's palette walk, made deterministic). Each is an integration avenue;
   together they deliver what the Ouarpeggiator set out to do, inside a better sequencer.

Recommended path ([§7](#7-recommended-staging)): first what needs no new screen (key-aware spelling, a catalogue table
generated by the CPG's own modules), then the catalogue in the existing PROGRESSIONS browser, then the palette in the bar
editor, then the palette walk. Before any of it, settle the governance question of [§8](#8-licence-and-governance):
personal fork, upstream contribution, or a fork used as a demonstrator for upstream. I recommend the third.

## 2. The three projects, in the terms that matter here

### 2.1 Cadenza

**Architecture.** `src/` is a host-free C engine: [seq_core.c](../../src/seq_core.c) (10 271 lines) with theory, gen,
rhythm, bassline, melody2, voicing, jamlib and feel. [engine.c](../../src/engine.c) binds it to the
[mpc-vst-plugins](https://github.com/open-mpc/mpc-vst-plugins) VST2 wrapper and opens its own ALSA MIDI port;
[vst/make_skin.py](../../vst/make_skin.py) renders the skin. The same engine runs the iOS app and the browser version
([cadenzajam.com](https://cadenzajam.com)): several 0.27 and 0.28 features are marked "iOS first" in
[seq_core.h](../../src/seq_core.h) and have no Force screen.

**Harmony model.** A part (song section) is a loop of 1, 2, 4, 8 or 16 bars ([seq_core.h:194](../../src/seq_core.h#L194))
of 16 steps a bar. Its chords are *slots* of `chord_len` (two bars down to a quarter bar), at most 64
([seq_core.h:23](../../src/seq_core.h#L23)), each holding:

- a degree 0-6 of the project scale;
- a source, `src`: 0 the project scale, 1-3 one of the scale's three borrowed chords (a fixed table per scale,
  [theory.c:122](../../src/theory.c#L122)), 4-51 a chromatic chord, 12 roots by four qualities, major, minor, dominant,
  diminished ([theory.h:50-55](../../src/theory.h#L50));
- a colour: Triad, 7th, sus2, sus4, add9, Power, 9th, 11th, 13th, 6th ([theory.h:39](../../src/theory.h#L39)), its tones
  taken from the chord's own scale.

On top of the slots: up to four change points a bar (a chord change inside a bar), and since 0.28 the Feel layer per slot
(a mute, the chord as first written, hand-placed notes, a voicing recipe).

**The property that makes integration cheap.** Steps are chord-relative: a step stores a tone above the root of the chord
under it (TONE_R to TONE_7) and an octave, and the bass and melody models read the chord under each step
([seq_core.h:81](../../src/seq_core.h#L81), [melody2.h](../../src/melody2.h)). Change the slots and the three lanes
follow, borrowed, chromatic and suspended chords included. Cadenza treats harmony as an *input* of a generative arranger;
feeding it a richer harmony is exactly what the architecture invites. The observation that prompted this study, a
CHORDS page built around "Progression" and "Voicing", is correct and goes deeper than the page: every lane is derived
from the slot list.

**Progressions.** 16 presets of at most 8 diatonic degrees ([theory.c:12](../../src/theory.c#L12)), named by nickname
(Epic, Pop, Sad Pop, 50s ... Canon (8)), shown in a browser of four rows, POP, MOODY, CLASSIC, LOOPS. A seeded generator
exists ([gen.c:1142](../../src/gen.c#L1142)): it starts on the tonic, weights degrees by tiers of a HARMONY value, ends on
a chord that leads home as the loop wraps, and adds at most one borrowed chord above HARMONY 60. Its HARMONY knob and its
NEW PROGRESSION button appear on no 0.28 Force screen ([vst/layout.conf](../../vst/layout.conf) has neither).

**Voicing.** Four connection modes (Close, Smooth, Top line, Rootless), the COLOUR, INVERSION, SPREAD (Cluster to Wide),
STRUM and OCTAVE knobs, 52 named styles in 13 genres ([voicing.c](../../src/voicing.c)). Smooth chooses, among the
inversions shifted by an octave either way with the lowest note in MIDI 43-62, the one with the least total
displacement.

**Names.** Every pitch is spelled with a sharp ([theory.c:6](../../src/theory.c#L6)). Measured: F major's IV reads `A#`,
E♭ major is shown as `D#` with chords `D# Fm Gm G# A# Cm Ddim`, and a chromatic ♭II7 in C is named `C#7` while its
numeral reads `bII7`. Numerals count from the project's own scale: A minor reads `i ii° III iv v VI VII`, where the CPG
writes ♭III, ♭VI and ♭VII.

**Invariants.** Nothing is random: outputs derive from seeds and hashes, and checksums pin them
([tests/core_parity.sha256](../../tests/core_parity.sha256); the comment on `theory_voice_next` in
[theory.h](../../src/theory.h) forbids changing how saved projects sound). Enumerations are append-only because they are
saved as digits; new behaviour gets a new identifier. Key, scale, MIDI channels and the PLAY knobs are shared by all six
song parts. The project state is text, at most 8 KB per instance ([seq_core.h:34](../../src/seq_core.h#L34)).

**Budgets** ([vst/budget.json](../../vst/budget.json)), every raise approved by the owner after measurement: skin images
78.8 MB decoded against 80, and binary 666 KB against 688, at their last recorded measurements; audio p99 24.2 % of a
block in the latest bench
([vst/bench.txt](../../vst/bench.txt)) against 49; 2 037 parameters. Progression changes run in setters, off the audio
thread: a richer harmony engine costs no audio time, but it costs binary, parameters and skin.

**The public repository is a release export.** Each release arrives as one commit titled "public release export"; the
development history stays private. Referenced but absent: `tools/make_presets.*` (which generates [presets_data.h](../../src/presets_data.h)), `tools/textfit.py`,
`tests/ref/*.js`, the web and iOS parity tests. The offline engine is nevertheless reproducible: the core parity trace,
built in this container with gcc 13 on x86-64, matches the pinned hash (`934d3f5c...`). The plugin test needs the
toolchain branch [`chordseq/open-mpc`](https://github.com/Shrekage2024/mpc-vst-plugins/tree/chordseq/open-mpc).

### 2.2 The Chord Progression Generator

**Data.** 184 template entries in 22 genres; 152 distinct values, 19 of which appear under several genres with
priorities of their own. A strict grammar ([musicTheory.js](https://github.com/liotier/AkaiMPC/blob/main/AkaiMPCChordProgressionGenerator/modules/musicTheory.js),
`parseChordToken`) reads accidental, stem case and suffix (`♭II7`, `V7/ii`, `i(maj7)`, `i♯5`) into a root in semitones
above the tonic and a chord type from a vocabulary of 50. 121 entries carry palette priorities (preferred, allowed and
avoided chord types), 6 a hard palette filter, 22 a row-4 hint (chromatic mediants, mode flips). Nicknames and
descriptions exist in six languages. Scale Mode offers 34 scales.

**Engine** ([generation.js](https://github.com/liotier/AkaiMPC/blob/main/AkaiMPCChordProgressionGenerator/modules/generation.js),
`generateVariant`): template, then chords, then a 16-pad palette.

- Rows 1-3: the progression's chords, then extensions per degree (7, M7, 9 depending on the style), the colours the genre
  declares, complements (ii and vi in major; iiø7 and v in minor), the other mode's colour chords (♭VII, ♭VI, ♭III, iv),
  sorted by priority then by "spice", from foundation to spicy. No two pads share a sound.
- Row 4: candidates scored by the harmonic functions the grid lacks, root motion by semitone or fifth, common usage,
  variety and the template's preferences. The pool: borrowed chords, V7, ii7, iv, VI, secondary dominants (V7/V, V7/ii,
  V7/vi, V7/IV; in minor V7/iv, V7/♭III, V7/♭VI, V7/♭VII), the leading-tone seventh, augmented sixths, the Neapolitan,
  the tritone substitute, the Lydian II, chromatic mediants and the hexatonic pole, mode flips.

Six voicing treatments: Smooth (a scored search rewarding common tones, stepwise and contrary motion, penalising leaps
and range), Classic (least total motion by Hungarian assignment over the inversions and a dropped top voice), Jazz
(close, sevenths added from pad 5), Modal (drop-2), Experimental (spread), Pedal (the first chord's bass held under every
chord that contains it,
[spec 11](https://github.com/liotier/AkaiMPC/blob/main/AkaiMPCChordProgressionGenerator/docs/improvements/11-pedal-voicing-style.md)).
Spelling follows key and degree: F♯m in A major, never G♭m
([spec 13](https://github.com/liotier/AkaiMPC/blob/main/AkaiMPCChordProgressionGenerator/docs/improvements/13-spelling.md)).
Scale Mode lays out 16 distinct chords of any scale; the Chord Matcher infers keys from chords.

**Apparatus.** `generation.js` and `musicTheory.js` are free of the DOM and run under Node. CI runs a theory audit and
diffs a committed [catalogue snapshot](https://github.com/liotier/AkaiMPC/blob/main/AkaiMPCChordProgressionGenerator/docs/catalogue-snapshot.txt).
This matters here: a table for Cadenza can be *generated by the CPG's own code* instead of being transcribed.

**Output.** `.progression` JSON for Pad Perform (`name`, `rootNote`, `scale`, `recordingOctave`, and 16 chords each with
`name`, `role` and MIDI `notes`; [export specification](https://github.com/liotier/AkaiMPC/blob/main/AkaiMPCChordProgressionGenerator/docs/mpc-export-specification.md)),
and Standard MIDI Files. No durations.

### 2.3 The Ouarpeggiator

A browser arpeggiator over whole progressions, built on a copy of the CPG's theory module: Euclidean rhythms (Bjorklund's
algorithm, after [Toussaint 2005](http://cgm.cs.mcgill.ca/~godfried/publications/banff.pdf): hits, steps, rotation, and a
free-running polymeter mode), note orders (up, down, up-down, converge, diverge, random...), octave spread, swing,
humanisation, velocity and gate shaped by curves, and a stab mode whose chord changes follow their own Euclidean pattern.

Its harmonic idea is the **palette walk**: the chord order either follows the template or walks the palette, each next pad
chosen by a score (40 % common tones, 40 % voice-leading distance, 20 % root motion by a fifth), sharpened by an
"adherence" exponent and optionally steered toward the smallest or the largest motion
([sequencerCore.js](https://github.com/liotier/Ouarpeggiator/blob/main/js/sequencerCore.js),
`calculateHarmonicScore` and `selectNextChordHarmonically` in
[musicTheory.js](https://github.com/liotier/Ouarpeggiator/blob/main/js/modules/musicTheory.js)).

Two observations:

- It draws on `Math.random`, so a pattern one liked cannot be recalled. Cadenza's founding rule is the opposite.
- Its copy of the theory module, annotated "shared verbatim with the AkaiMPC generator - do not fork", has drifted: 173
  templates and five styles against the CPG's 184 and six. A third copy, in C and maintained by hand, would drift the same
  way. [Avenue B](#b-a-catalogue-table-generated-by-the-cpg) is designed to prevent that.

Against Cadenza, the rhythmic machinery is the Ouarpeggiator's weaker half: Cadenza already has phrase forms, call and
answer, chord-tone-aware lines, interlocking bass and melody, locks, song parts and placement from MPC's song position.
What the Ouarpeggiator has and Cadenza lacks: Euclidean patterns, polymeter, curve-shaped dynamics, the palette walk.
Only the last is harmonic, and it is the piece worth carrying over.

## 3. Complementarity, measured

| | Cadenza 0.28.1, as the Force's screens expose it | CPG |
|---|---|---|
| Progressions | 16, at most 8 diatonic degrees | 184 entries (152 distinct) in 22 genres, 1 to 12 chords |
| Chords offered while editing | the scale's 7 and 3 borrowed; types sus2, sus4, add9, 6 | a 16-pad palette per template, weighted by genre, with a functional fourth row |
| Chromatic chords | in the engine since 0.28, no Force screen | secondary dominants, Neapolitan, augmented sixths, tritone substitute, mediants, mode flips |
| Scales | 7 | 34, of which 23 heptatonic |
| Voicing | 4 modes, 5 knobs, 52 named styles | 6 treatments |
| Spelling | sharps only | by key and degree |
| Time | rhythm, phrase, three lanes, song parts | none |
| Reproducibility | seeded; checksums pin the output | pure functions; the Ouarpeggiator uses `Math.random` |

**Coverage.** Every chord Cadenza's theory layer can voice was enumerated (method in Appendix A) and every CPG template was
rendered by the CPG's own modules, assigned the Cadenza scale holding most of its chords diatonically, and each chord
classified.

Template chords, repeats included (745):

| Class | Chords | Share |
|---|---|---|
| diatonic in the template's best Cadenza scale | 639 | 85.8 % |
| one of that scale's three borrowed chords (`src` 1-3) | 49 | 6.6 % |
| chromatic chord of the Feel layer (`src` 4-51) | 54 | 7.2 % |
| near: same root, sets differing only by the perfect fifth | 2 | 0.3 % |
| not representable | 1 | 0.1 % |

| Templates (184) | Count |
|---|---|
| entirely diatonic in their best Cadenza scale | 109 |
| reachable through today's progression path (diatonic and borrowed only) | 144 |
| reachable through the Feel layer (chromatic and near included) | 183 |

Best Cadenza scale per template: Major 85, Minor 63, Mixolydian 13, Dorian 11, Lydian 5, Harmonic minor 5, Phrygian 2.

Palette pads, Classic variant (184 × 16 = 2 944): 52.9 % diatonic, 12.6 % borrowed, 29.4 % chromatic, 3.8 % near,
1.4 % not representable. The near pads are the Italian sixth (98, which Cadenza would voice as a full ♭VI7, the German
sixth's sound, a family the CPG itself treats as one), 13th chords without their fifth (10), minor ♯5 (2), a minor 11th
(1). The absent ones: quartal triads (31), minor-major sevenths (3), 7♭5 (2), half-diminished sevenths where the scale
has none (2), 7♭9 (1), a minor sixth (1).

Two caveats. "Near" is a pitch-class judgement: the line cliché i-i♯5-i6-i♯5 counts as near, yet is musically lost,
because the added fifth stops the moving voice the progression exists for. And the template chords use only nine of the
50 types (major, minor, dom7, major7, minor7, diminished, minMaj7, minor6, minAug): the CPG's harmonic wealth lies in its
roots and its palettes, not in exotic chord types.

**Lengths.** 145 of the 184 entries have 1, 2, 4 or 8 chords. 39 do not: 23 have 3, 8 have 5, 3 have 6, 4 have 7, one
(the 12-bar blues) has 12.

**Reading.** The vocabulary gap is marginal. What separates the CPG's harmony from the Force's screens is a write path, a
time grid, screen space and a licence.

## 4. Constraints any integration must respect

1. **Determinism and saved sound.** A saved project must sound the same after an upgrade. New behaviour takes a new
   identifier (a new voicing mode, a new generator version); existing outputs are pinned by checksums. The CPG is pure as
   well, so whatever is generated from it is reproducible by construction; the palette walk must be seeded, not random.
2. **One engine, three products.** `seq_core` serves the Force, the iOS app and the browser version. Upstream, any engine
   change crosses the author's commercial products and parity tests this export does not contain.
3. **The Force's budgets.** At most 1.2 MB of skin headroom: a new full-panel view has cost 1.6 MB or more by the
   budget's own history, so screens must reuse existing views. At most 22 KB of binary headroom. Parameters: each new tile is a
   parameter, and the bench's random sweep grows with them. The owner approves every raise upstream; in a fork the
   numbers still describe a real device.
4. **The time grid.** Loops of 1, 2, 4, 8 or 16 bars ("128 divides by every loop length",
   [MANUAL §3](../../MANUAL.md#3-set-up-a-project)); 16 steps a bar; Feel chords resize by half or quarter bars; at most four
   change points a bar and 64 slots a loop.
5. **The save format.** `.ch` stores `src` 0-3 only ([seq_core.c:3886](../../src/seq_core.c#L3886)); chromatic chords live
   in the 0.28 `.cx` field, their `src` written as two hex digits ([seq_core.c:3935](../../src/seq_core.c#L3935)). The Feel "base" code,
   `1 + deg + 7 × (src + 52 × colour)` ([seq_core.c:530](../../src/seq_core.c#L530)), bakes `THEORY_SRC_MAX + 1 = 52` into
   saved projects: enlarging `CQ_COUNT` to add chord qualities would renumber every saved chromatic chord and misread
   every saved base. New qualities need an appended second range and a frozen multiplier.
6. **Global key and scale.** All six song parts share key and scale; a template cannot bring its own mode into one part.
   The lines take their non-chord tones from the project scale (in [melody2.h](../../src/melody2.h) only the chord-tone
   slots read the chord; in [bassline.h](../../src/bassline.h) a passing note is a scale step, only the approach is
   chromatic), so a minor template in a major project gets correct chords and clashing passing notes (E naturals over Cm
   in C major).
7. **One source of truth for harmony: the CPG.** Cadenza should consume generated data, never a hand-kept translation
   (the Ouarpeggiator's drift, §2.3).
8. **The licence** ([§8](#8-licence-and-governance)).

## 5. Mapping the CPG onto Cadenza

### 5.1 Chords

Each CPG chord is a root `r` (semitones above the tonic) and a type `T`. Against the project scale, in order of
preference:

1. **Diatonic:** a degree whose root is `r` and whose chord in some colour sounds exactly `T` → (degree, `src` 0, colour).
2. **Borrowed:** one of the scale's three borrowed chords → (degree, `src` 1-3, colour).
3. **Chromatic:** `src = 4 + 4r + quality`, colour → through the Feel layer.
4. **Near:** the closest of the above differing only by the fifth, the loss recorded.
5. **Absent:** refuse the template, or substitute and say so.

| CPG type | Cadenza (quality, colour) | Note |
|---|---|---|
| major, minor | (major or minor, Triad) | |
| dom7, major7, minor7 | (dominant, 7th), (major, 7th), (minor, 7th) | |
| diminished, dim7 | (diminished, Triad), (diminished, 7th) | |
| sus2, sus4, add9, minAdd9 | colours sus2, sus4, add9 on the right quality | |
| major6 | (major, 6th) | |
| minor6 | Dorian or Melodic-minor degree only | the chromatic minor is Aeolian: its 6th is ♭6 |
| dom9, major9, minor9 | 9th on the right quality | |
| 11th, 13th | 11th, 13th | Cadenza omits the fifth: near; a minor 13th needs a Dorian degree (the chromatic minor gives ♭13) |
| m7♭5 | a diatonic degree only (ii of minor, vii of major) | no chromatic half-diminished |
| augmented, augMaj7 | Harmonic minor's III+ only (Triad, 7th) | no chromatic augmented quality |
| minMaj7 | Harmonic minor's i only (7th) | no chromatic minor-major quality |
| aug7 | none | |
| It+6, Ger+6 | (dominant, 7th) on ♭VI | It+6 near, Ger+6 exact |
| quartal, 7sus4, 7♭5, 7♭9, 7♯9, alt, minor ♯5 | none | [avenue H](#h-completing-the-chord-vocabulary) |

### 5.2 Which scale

Two policies:

- **Respect the project's scale** and map whatever is foreign as borrowed or chromatic. Honest, and the only policy that
  respects the other five parts; but a minor template in a major project inherits clashing passing notes (§4.6).
- **Switch the project's scale** to the template's best one. Coherent lines, but it changes every part.

Recommendation: respect the scale, and let the browser show by default the templates of the matching family (major
tonality for Major, Lydian and Mixolydian; minor tonality for Minor, Dorian, Phrygian and Harmonic minor), with an
explicit "all" view and a one-tap "switch the song to this template's scale". The measured best-scale distribution says
the default view loses little: the families coincide with the CPG's own tonality field for nearly all templates.

### 5.3 Harmonic rhythm

The CPG gives no durations; Cadenza needs them. For the 145 entries of length 1, 2, 4 or 8, one chord a bar (or the
part's CHORD LENGTH) is the obvious default. For the other 39, the choice is musical, not technical:

| Layout of 3 chords in 4 bars | Effect |
|---|---|
| 1-1-2 (the last chord held) | the arrival breathes: ii-V-I with its I held, as jazz writes it |
| 2-1-1 (the first chord held) | the key is stated before the motion: i-♭VI-♭VII |
| 1-1-1 and a fourth chord | changes the template; unacceptable without saying so |

Five chords in eight bars can lay out 2-2-2-1-1, 1-1-1-1-4, and so on. Feel stores any of these layouts on its
quarter-bar grid.

Measured, the tonic decides most cases. Of the 38 odd-length entries (the 12-bar blues aside), 15 end on the tonic
(ii-V-I, ♭VI-♭VII-I, iv-♭VII-I, I-IV-I-IV-I-V-I...), 21 start on it without ending on it (i-♭VI-♭VII, i-iv-v,
I-V-vi-iii-IV...), and 2 do neither. Hence the recommended default: **give the spare time to the tonic**, held at the
end when the template ends on it, at the start when it only starts on it, and to the last chord otherwise.

The durable answer lies upstream of Cadenza: an optional harmonic-rhythm field in the CPG's templates (the 12-bar blues
is already, implicitly, a template with durations), which would also serve the CPG's own MIDI export.

### 5.4 The 12-bar blues

Twelve bars are not a Cadenza loop length. Three ways out, none free: two song parts of 8 and 4 bars chained by the 0.27
song order (which has no Force screen); a 12-bar loop length (an engine-wide change, and 12 does not divide MPC's default
128-bar sequence, so the sequence-loop advice of the manual breaks); or leaving it out of the Force catalogue. I would
leave it out until the song order has a screen.

### 5.5 Voicings, names, numerals

- **Import chord identity, not CPG voicings.** Cadenza re-voices from chord tones with its own modes and knobs, which are
  more expressive than the CPG's treatments. The exception is a `.progression` import
  ([avenue J](#j-progression-import-and-export)), where Feel's hand notes can preserve a pad's exact notes.
- **Names on tiles, not numerals.** The CPG reads numerals against the parallel major, Cadenza against the project scale
  (§2.1). The existing browser already shows names in the key ("Epic · Am F C G"); keeping names sidesteps the conflict.
  Wherever numerals do appear, they should follow Cadenza's convention, consistent with the rest of its screens.

## 6. Integration avenues

Sizes follow the CPG's improvement specifications: **S** under a day of a session's work with review, **M** one to two,
**L** a multi-step job with a checkpoint review.

### A. Key-aware chord spelling

- **What.** Port the CPG's spelling rules (spec 13): a root takes the letter of its degree, a two-named tonic takes the
  spelling with fewer accidentals, chord tones take letters from their degrees. ASCII as today (`Bb`, the Force's font
  has no ♭).
- **Value.** Fixes a visible defect on every screen in every flat key: F major's IV becomes `Bb`, E♭ major stops
  reading `D#`, the chromatic ♭II7 becomes `Db7` next to its `bII7`.
- **Engine.** Display only: names are cached labels, never saved. Touches
  [theory.c](../../src/theory.c) (`theory_chord_name`, `theory_chord_name_c`, `theory_chord_card`, the key names) and
  the keyboard's note labels. Width checks: `Dbm7b5` is as long as `C#m7b5`.
- **Fit.** Self-contained, useful to the iOS and browser versions as well: the best first contribution upstream.
- **Size.** S to M.

### B. A catalogue table generated by the CPG

- **What.** A Node tool importing the CPG's modules and emitting a C header: per template its genre, English nickname,
  tonality, best Cadenza scale, chords as (degree, `src`, colour) or (root, quality, colour), default durations, and the
  palette's extra pads with their spice level and category. A CI check regenerates it and fails on a difference, as the
  CPG's catalogue snapshot does.
- **Value.** No screen by itself, but everything else consumes it, and it makes the CPG the single source of truth.
- **Budget.** About 152 templates × at most 12 chords × 3 bytes, plus nicknames and palettes: roughly 8 to 12 KB of
  binary, within the 22 KB headroom.
- **Where the tool lives.** In the CPG repository, it would be one more consumer of `generation.js` beside
  `build-pack.mjs`; in this repository, it would import a pinned CPG commit. Question 6 of [§9](#9-open-questions).
- **Size.** S to M.

### C. The catalogue in the PROGRESSIONS browser

- **What.** The existing browser ([layout.conf:133-141](../../vst/layout.conf#L133)) has 16 tiles, DONE, CANCEL and a
  title readout; its row labels POP, MOODY, CLASSIC, LOOPS are static text. Replace them with a genre selector (a
  readout with arrows, or the VOICING browser's genre list, whose 15 tiles cannot hold 22 genres without paging) and page
  the templates 16 at a time (Jazz/Functional has 22, Film/Cinematic 18). PROGRESSION ◀ ▶ on the CHORDS page steps
  through the current genre. Tiles keep today's format, "nickname · chords in the key", cut today at 19 characters
  (`BPR_CHARS`, [engine.c:596](../../src/engine.c#L596)): long templates show their nickname alone.
- **Engine.** Generalise `seq_prog_try` and `seq_prog_step` to take a template whose chords may be chromatic and of unequal
  length, written through Feel's store (which already keeps the loop's length and handles both), keeping slot locks as
  today. Generalise the "preset shown" match.
- **Budget.** The 16 tile parameters exist; a few for the genre selector. If the view's image is unchanged, no new skin
  image: to be confirmed with the skin gauge, since `tools/textfit.py` is not in the export.
- **Value.** The highest of all: the CPG's core on the Force, every lane following.
- **Size.** M to L.

### D. The palette wherever a chord is chosen

- **What.** The CHORDS bar editor offers the scale's seven chords and three borrowed ones. Add the CPG palette of the
  part's template as a third source, showing its secondary dominants, Neapolitan, tritone substitute, augmented sixths
  (as ♭VI7), mediants and mode flips, coloured by spice. Cadenza's drift legend (green close to red dissonant) already
  speaks that language.
- **A harmonic VARIATION.** Cadenza's VARIATION varies rhythm and line at phrase ends. Its harmonic counterpart would
  substitute, at bar 4 or bars 2 and 4, the last chord by a palette chord of one category (a V7, a V7/x, the tritone
  substitute, a borrowed iv): a deterministic turnaround generator, which is what the CPG's fourth row is for.
- **Two ways to get palettes.** Precomputed per catalogue template (data, a few KB, no drift), or the palette builder
  ported to C so that any progression gets one, hand-made ones included (its analysis takes any chord list: roughly a
  thousand lines of JavaScript comments included, perhaps 10 to 15 KB of binary, kept honest by a parity test against the
  catalogue snapshot). Start with the first.
- **Screen.** The bar editor is dense: a source toggle on the BORROW row (SCALE · BORROW · PALETTE) costs less than a new
  row.
- **Size.** M.

### E. The palette walk, deterministic

- **What.** The Ouarpeggiator's selection rule (common tones, voice-leading distance, fifth motion, adherence) as a new
  seeded generator version beside `gen_progression_v2`, constrained like it: start on the tonic, end on a chord that
  leads home as the loop wraps, keep locked slots. The palette is the template's, or the union of the genre's.
- **Value.** Endless progressions in a genre's idiom, each one recallable, the same seed giving the same walk. This is the
  Ouarpeggiator's ambition, inside a sequencer that already knows what to do with the result.
- **Save.** Nothing new: progressions are saved as slots, and the generator's golden is "not save-critical" by its own
  header ([gen.h](../../src/gen.h)).
- **Screen.** A NEW tile in the browser and the HARMONY value somewhere visible; the eight CHORDS knobs are taken.
- **Size.** M.

### F. A Pedal voicing mode

- **What.** The CPG's Pedal treatment as a fifth voicing mode: the part's first bass note held at the bottom of every
  chord that contains it, the other chords voiced above it. Distinct from the BASS page's PEDAL, which acts on the bass
  lane. New voicing styles can use it (the CPG's example: Em, C, A over a held E).
- **Rule.** Append the mode; leave Smooth alone (its output is pinned). The CPG's richer Smooth scoring (common tones,
  stepwise and contrary motion) could become a sixth mode, under the same rule.
- **Size.** S to M.

### G. A genre crosswalk, and presets

- **What.** Map the CPG's 22 genres onto Cadenza's genre vocabularies: 6 JAM columns, 13 voicing genres, 12 bass genres.
  Italo-Disco/House → HOUSE and DISCO, voicing House, bass House; Trance/Psytrance/Goa → TRANCE; Jungle/Drum'n'Bass →
  voicing and bass Drum & Bass, no JAM column; Film/Cinematic → Film, Film / Ambient. Picking a template could then
  offer, without imposing, a matching voicing style and bass style.
- **Presets.** Cadenza's preset index is capped at 16 ([seq_core.h:588](../../src/seq_core.h#L588)), each preset fills
  all six song parts, and the tool that bakes them is absent from the export: presets are a vehicle for curation, not for
  a catalogue.
- **Size.** M.

### H. Completing the chord vocabulary

- **What.** Half-diminished and minor-major as chromatic qualities, augmented, 7sus4 (and the quartal triad as its fifthless
  form), 7♭5 and 7♭9, the minor ♯5 of the line cliché. Templates reach 184 of 184 and pads nearly all.
- **How.** A second chromatic range appended after `src` 51, each quality a seven-note scale like the existing chromatic
  rows (Locrian for ø, melodic minor for mMaj7 and m6, an augmented scale for +); 7sus4 as an appended colour. Names and
  numerals must learn them. The Feel code multiplier stays 52 for old codes (§4.5).
- **Fit.** Shared engine and save format: upstream-sensitive.
- **Size.** M.

### I. More heptatonic scales

- **What.** Of the CPG's 34 scales, 23 have seven notes and fit Cadenza's seven-degree model; 12 of them are new to
  Cadenza once duplicates are removed (Kafi is Dorian, Yaman Lydian, Bhairavi Phrygian, Bhairav Double harmonic): Locrian,
  Melodic minor, Double harmonic, Hungarian minor, Neapolitan major and minor, Phrygian dominant, Persian, Todi, Lydian
  dominant, Altered, Locrian ♯2. Each needs its three borrowed chords chosen. The eleven others (the major and minor
  pentatonics, blues, whole tone, the two octatonics, the augmented scale, Hirajoshi, Insen, Kumoi and the Egyptian
  pentatonic) break the seven-degree assumption that runs through the engine: out of scope.
- **Caveat inherited.** The CPG presents its ragas and exotic scales as twelve-tone approximations, to be treated "as
  flavour, not as authority"; Cadenza should say the same.
- **Screen.** GLOBAL's SCALE selector grows from 7 entries.
- **Size.** M.

### J. `.progression` import and export

- **What.** Read Pad Perform files: identify each pad's chord from its notes (the interval analysis of the
  [Finder](https://github.com/liotier/AkaiMPC/tree/main/AkaiMPCChordProgressionFinder)), keep the exact voicing as Feel
  hand notes (up to six), decide which pads form the sequence (CPG files carry their numerals in `name`; others need a
  rule or a pick). Export the reverse: the part's chords as a `.progression`, so Pad Perform plays Cadenza's harmony.
- **Unknowns.** Akai documents a `Progressions` folder at the root of a storage device
  ([Akai support](https://support.akaipro.com/en/support/solutions/articles/69000824380-mpc-x-live-and-one-using-your-progressions-in-standalone));
  whether a plugin loaded through MPC OS 3.10.1's official plugin support may read and write there is unverified and needs
  a device probe. File I/O belongs in setters, never in `seq_block`. A file browser is the most screen-hungry piece of this
  study.
- **Value.** High for libraries of `.progression` files (Akai's factory ones included); lower once C exists.
- **Size.** L.

### K. Words

- **What.** CPG nicknames on tiles ("Axis of Awesome", "Andalusian", "Hotel California"); the CPG's plain-language chord
  phrase ("Dominant seventh chord on the flattened second degree") on the message line when a chord is picked. The
  descriptions are too long for a line that fades after four seconds. English only: Cadenza has no localisation, and the
  CPG's exports are canonical English anyway (D2 of its export specification).
- **Size.** S, once B exists.

### Not recommended now

- **The Euclidean engine and polymeter.** Rhythm features, not harmony; the second breaks the sixteen-steps-a-bar
  invariant. Cadenza's rhythm library already holds the most common Euclidean idiom (its Tresillo is E(3,8)) and its
  Latin relatives (Son Clave, Bossa). A future JAM column perhaps, competing for skin budget.
- **The Chord Matcher on the device.** Little use on a 7-inch screen where the key is one tap away on GLOBAL.
- **The CPG's six voicing treatments wholesale.** Cadenza's voicing is already more expressive; only Pedal adds something.
- **Anything flowing from Cadenza into the CPG** ([§8](#8-licence-and-governance)).

## 8. Licence and governance

**Cadenza's licence.** [PolyForm Shield 1.0.0](https://polyformproject.org/licenses/shield/1.0.0), with the notices in
[NOTICE](../../NOTICE): "Copyright (c) 2026 Shrey Sharma", and two declared lines of business, the Cadenza iOS app and the
Cadenza browser version. It permits use, modification and new works "for any permitted purpose", every purpose being
permitted "except for providing any product that competes with the software or any product the licensor or any of its
affiliates provides using the software". It adds that goods and services "compete even when provided free of charge".

My reading, which is not legal advice: modifying Cadenza for one's own use is clearly permitted; publishing a modified
Cadenza as an alternative plugin for others to install is close to the exclusion, since it would be a practical substitute
for Cadenza; and whatever would compete with the iOS or browser versions stays excluded even if they were discontinued,
because NOTICE declares them as lines of business (the licence's "Discontinued Products" clause).
Distribution also obliges the distributor to pass on the licence and the "Required Notice" line. This repository's name
signals a variant: the licensor should be asked before anyone else uses it.

**The CPG's and the Ouarpeggiator's licence.** The Unlicense: nothing prevents porting their data and algorithms into
Cadenza. Contributing them upstream means donating public-domain work to a source-available product; the CPG itself stays
public domain. The reverse direction, Cadenza's code into the CPG or the Ouarpeggiator, is what the noncompete clause
targets.

**Upstream practicalities.** The public repository receives one commit per release, the history staying private; budgets are raised
only by the owner after measurement; the engine serves three products, two of them commercial; the iOS Feel page already
picks chords on any root, so the author may prefer to bring catalogue and palette to iOS first. A pull request would
probably be re-implemented rather than merged. Proposals carry better than patches: a design note, a measured table, a
small fix.

**Options.**

| Option | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| Personal fork | fastest, full control | cannot be distributed; diverges from every upstream release |
| Upstream first | reaches every Cadenza user, iOS and browser included; no licence question | slower; the author decides scope and screens |
| Fork as demonstrator, upstream as destination | a working prototype on an MPC One argues better than a description; low-risk pieces go upstream early | requires structuring the work as separable units |

I recommend the third: contact the author before investing in screens, offer stage 1 first (the spelling fix and the
generated table are small, self-contained and useful to all three products), and add a field report while at it. The
README lists both the MPC One and MPC OS 3.10.1 as untested, and this fork's owner runs that combination daily: an entry
for [tested.json](../../tested.json) costs nothing and starts the conversation on useful ground.

## Appendix A: measurement method

All scripts were written in the session scratchpad and are not committed.

1. **Cadenza's chord space.** A C program linked against [src/theory.c](../../src/theory.c) enumerated, at key C, for each
   of the seven scales, every `src` from 0 to 51, the degrees each allows (any for `src` 0, the borrow's degree for 1-3,
   degree 0 for the chromatic chords) and the ten colours: 4 060 chords, each as root pitch class, pitch-class set and
   notes, from `theory_chord_notes`.
2. **The CPG's catalogue.** A Node script imported the CPG's `musicTheory.js` and `generation.js` at commit `c84a306`.
   Template chords came from `generateProgressionChords` (repeats kept, 745 chords); pads from `generateVariant('Classic')`
   in C (184 × 16). Roots: the parser's `scaleDegree` for template chords, the spelled chord name for pads.
3. **Classification.** For each template, the Cadenza scale maximising, in order, diatonic, borrowed, then chromatic
   chords was retained; each chord was matched on (root, pitch-class set). "Near": the same root and sets differing only by
   the perfect fifth, in either direction.
4. **Other figures.** Template lengths, genres, priorities, hints and chord types were counted from the CPG's
   `progressions` and `CHORD_INTERVALS`; Cadenza's spellings were printed by `theory_chord_name`, `theory_chord_name_c`,
   `theory_degree_c` and `theory_numeral`; the core parity trace was built from [tests/core_parity.c](../../tests/core_parity.c)
   and hashed.
